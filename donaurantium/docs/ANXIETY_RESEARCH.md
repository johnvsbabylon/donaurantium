## Gene Analysis

### FKBP5 (rs1360780) — The Stress Volume Knob
**Expression: ref=183.000 | ≈ neutral variant effect**

FKBP5 (FK506-binding protein 5) is the master regulator of glucocorticoid receptor (GR)
sensitivity and HPA axis feedback. When cortisol is released, it should bind GR, produce
its effects, then trigger negative feedback that turns cortisol production off. FKBP5 sits in
the middle of this circuit as a feedback regulator — it modulates GR's sensitivity to cortisol.

At 183.000, this is the **highest expression of any gene in the entire Donaurantium panel**
across all three conditions (anxiety, spine, depression). Higher than IL-1β (20.625). Higher
than the serotonin transporter SLC6A4 (124.0). The immune-stress interface is running at
maximum regulatory volume.

The T allele (rs1360780-T) = increased FKBP5 expression = blunted GR sensitivity = impaired
negative feedback = prolonged cortisol elevation after each stressor. This is the primary
gene × early adversity interaction in PTSD and anxiety research. The T allele creates a cortisol
system that turns on fast but turns off slowly — and each activation increases FKBP5 expression
further, creating a feed-forward loop.

**Documented acoustic mechanism:** Therapeutic rhythmic sound has been shown to increase DNA
methylation of the FKBP5 promoter — epigenetically silencing the stress amplifier. Noise stress
(>85 dB, disorganized) does the opposite: upregulates FKBP5 and deepens the feed-forward loop.
The protocol must be the exact inverse of noise stress: organized, rhythmic, low-amplitude, sine.

**Acoustic target: 6 Hz theta binaural**
- Theta entrainment drives HPA axis suppression via prefrontal-limbic pathway
- Same mechanism documented in meditation studies showing FKBP5 methylation
- Overlaps with NR3C1 target (depression panel) — one protocol, both cortisol genes
- Carrier: 200–240 Hz per Oster curve optimum
- References: PMC6172823 (meditation + FKBP5 methylation), multiple HPA-theta studies

### SLC6A4 (rs25531) — The Serotonin Transporter
**Expression: ref=124.000 | alt=124.500 | +0.4% neutral**

SLC6A4 encodes the serotonin transporter (SERT, 5-HTT) — the protein that SSRIs (Prozac,
Zoloft, Lexapro) block. SERT reuptakes serotonin from the synapse back into the presynaptic
neuron, terminating serotonin signaling. High SERT expression = faster serotonin clearance =
less serotonin available = depression and anxiety vulnerability.

At 124.000 in CD4+ T cells, SLC6A4 is genuinely highly expressed — T cells use serotonin
signaling for immune function, and SERT is part of that regulation. The 5-HTTLPR polymorphism
(the "short" allele, linked to rs25531-G) reduces SERT expression and is paradoxically
associated with increased amygdala reactivity and stress sensitivity in humans despite lower
transporter levels — demonstrating the complexity of serotonin circuit regulation.

**Critical interaction:** High FKBP5 (183.0) + high SLC6A4 (124.0) together = cortisol turns
on the stress response fast AND serotonin is cleared quickly. The two primary anxiety circuit
genes are both running high simultaneously.

**Acoustic target: 6 Hz theta binaural**
- Theta state mimics the brain state associated with SSRI-like serotonin preservation
- Reduces sympathetic drive that upregulates SLC6A4 under chronic stress
- Same theta needle as FKBP5 — converging targets
- The "calm" state produced by theta entrainment recreates the neurochemical environment
  where SERT expression downregulates naturally

### COMT (rs4680) — The Dopamine Regulator
**Expression: ref=7.594 | ≈ neutral variant effect**

COMT (Catechol-O-methyltransferase) degrades dopamine and norepinephrine in the prefrontal
cortex. The Val158Met polymorphism (rs4680 G>A) is one of the most studied variants in
psychiatric genetics. G allele (Val) = high COMT activity = fast dopamine breakdown = "warrior"
phenotype — stress-resistant, calm under pressure. A allele (Met) = low COMT activity = slow
dopamine breakdown = "worrier" phenotype — better cognitive performance, higher anxiety.

At 7.594, COMT is in the mid-range. The variant itself is neutral (+0.0%), meaning the ref and
alt alleles produce identical expression in CD4+ T cells — consistent with COMT being primarily
relevant through its enzymatic activity (which differs between Val/Met), not through expression
level differences.

**Acoustic target: 40 Hz gamma + 10 Hz alpha**
- 40 Hz GENUS supports PFC regulatory circuits where COMT is most active
- 10 Hz alpha entrainment supports the prefrontal-to-limbic top-down control pathway
- Reducing noise stress (the primary COMT disruptor) preserves enzymatic function

### HTR2A (rs6311) — The Rumination Receptor
**Expression: ref=4.469 | alt=4.438 | -0.7% neutral**

HTR2A encodes the serotonin 2A receptor — the primary target of serotonergic psychedelics
(psilocybin, LSD) and one of the most studied receptors in depression and anxiety. HTR2A
activation promotes rumination and is heavily implicated in the default mode network (DMN) —
the self-referential thought loop that generates ruminative anxiety when chronically overactive.

A allele (rs6311-A) = higher HTR2A expression = more 5-HT2A activity = more DMN engagement =
more rumination, more negative emotional bias, more anxiety sensitivity.

**Acoustic target: 6 Hz theta binaural**
- Theta entrainment suppresses default mode network activity via the same mechanism
  as meditation and psilocybin-assisted therapy
- This is one of the most robust findings in psychoacoustics: theta states correlate with
  reduced DMN connectivity on fMRI
- The "quieting of the self-referential chatter" in deep meditation IS theta-driven DMN
  suppression, and binaural beats can induce this state without pharmacology

### BDNF (rs6265) — Neuroplasticity Anchor
**Expression: ref=2.062 | ≈ neutral variant effect**

BDNF (Brain-derived neurotrophic factor) Val66Met is the final common pathway of virtually
every effective antidepressant and anxiolytic intervention. Higher BDNF = more neuroplasticity
= better stress resilience = capacity to form new anxiety-free patterns. Met allele (T) =
impaired activity-dependent BDNF secretion — the brain releases less BDNF in response to
learning and activity.

At 2.062 in CD4+ T cells, BDNF shows lower baseline expression than the serotonin/stress
genes — consistent with its primary role in neural tissue rather than immune cells.

**Acoustic target: 20 Hz binaural + 40 Hz gamma**
- 20 Hz is the single strongest documented BDNF-upregulating frequency (PMC11316700)
- 40 Hz gamma activates neurogenesis via TCF4 pathway (MIT GENUS studies)
- BDNF is a cross-panel target: anxiety, depression, and spine panels all converge here
- One frequency (20 Hz) hits BDNF across all three conditions simultaneously

### GABRA2 (rs279858) — The Nearly Absent Brake
**Expression: ref=0.165 | alt=0.163 | -1.2% neutral**

GABRA2 encodes the alpha-2 subunit of the GABA-A receptor — the brain's primary inhibitory
ion channel. GABA is the braking system of the nervous system. Without adequate GABA-A receptor
expression, the brain cannot effectively dampen excitatory activity. This is why benzodiazepines,
alcohol, and THC are chemically anxiolytic — they all potentiate GABA-A.

At 0.165, GABRA2 is the lowest-expressed gene in the anxiety panel. In CD4+ T cells, this
makes sense — these are immune cells, not neurons. GABA-A receptors are their secondary
function. But the **ratio to FKBP5 (183.0 vs 0.165 = 1,109×)** is the core diagnostic signal:
the stress accelerator dwarfs the inhibitory system by over three orders of magnitude.

**Acoustic target: 10 Hz alpha binaural (primary) + 40 Hz gamma**
- Alpha binaural beats are the most documented acoustic GABAergic intervention
- Multiple studies show alpha entrainment increases GABA concentration in visual and
  sensorimotor cortex (Muthukumaraswamy et al., 2013)
- GABRA2 expression is linked to beta/low-gamma oscillation power — acoustic entrainment
  may drive compensatory upregulation
- 40 Hz gamma requires GABA interneurons (PV+ cells) for its expression — the GENUS
  protocol indirectly demands GABA-A function to sustain gamma oscillations
- References: PMC3652411 (alpha GABA), Muthukumaraswamy 2013, Bhatt et al.

## The "Startle-Sensitive" System Note

With FKBP5 at 183.0 and SLC6A4 at 124.0, this system is biologically tuned to respond
rapidly and strongly to unexpected stimuli. Any sudden sound, amplitude jump, or tonal
discontinuity in the session will trigger the very stress response the protocol is designed
to suppress.

**Non-negotiable implementation requirements:**
- **Minimum 120-second fade-in** on all carriers at session start
- **Cubic easing** (never linear, never hard cut) on all phase transitions
- **Sine waves only** — any harmonic distortion adds unexpected spectral content
- **No sudden amplitude changes** throughout the session
- **Soft landing**: 60-second fade-out at session end, not abrupt stop

These are not aesthetic choices. They are mechanistically necessary for a system with this
FKBP5/SLC6A4 expression profile.
