## PART I — THE SHARED UPSTREAM: WHY ONE CONDITION FEEDS ALL THREE

### The Bidirectional Loop

The comorbidity between chronic pain, anxiety, and depression is not coincidental.
It is mechanistic. Each condition drives the other two via shared molecular
pathways:

```
CHRONIC PAIN
    ↓ (pain → stress → HPA activation)
ELEVATED CORTISOL + SYMPATHETIC TONE
    ↓ (cortisol → immune dysregulation)
NEUROINFLAMMATION (IL-1β ↑, IL-6 ↑, TNF-α ↑)
    ↓ (IL-1β crosses blood-brain barrier)
DEPRESSION (TPH2 suppressed, MAOA upregulated, DRD3 circuit quieted)
    ↓ (depressed state → threat sensitivity ↑)
ANXIETY (FKBP5 sensitized, GABRA2 brake lost, amygdala hyperreactive)
    ↓ (anxiety → vigilance → pain amplification)
BACK TO CHRONIC PAIN (amplified)
```

This is not a metaphor. It is the confirmed molecular mechanism
(PMC10213648, PMC11875130, PMC8863453, PMC11764837):

- IL-1β mediates chronic stress-induced hyperalgesia — the stress that
  comes from depression and anxiety directly amplifies pain via the same
  inflammatory cytokine that drives spinal degeneration (PMC10052634)
- Higher plasma IL-1β, IL-6, and TNF-α are "most consistently" elevated
  in major depressive disorder (PMC12024626)
- Chronic pain triggers depression via HPA axis dysregulation and
  neuroinflammation; depressive states amplify pain sensitivity through
  neural plasticity changes — a self-perpetuating cycle (PubMed 27344405)
- FKBP5 — the anxiety panel's loudest gene at 183.0 — determines cortisol
  sensitivity, and cortisol directly suppresses TPH2 (depression) and
  upregulates spinal IL-1β expression (spine)

**The implication:** IL-1β suppression is not a spine intervention.
It is a unified intervention. Suppressing IL-1β reduces spinal
inflammation, de-suppresses TPH2 serotonin synthesis, and removes a
key driver of the NLRP3 inflammasome activation that sustains anxiety's
neural hyperreactivity — all simultaneously.
